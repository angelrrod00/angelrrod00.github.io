## Background and inspiration

During 2016 I became a Peer Academic Leader (PAL) at the STEM lab on Miami Dade College's Wolfson Campus. After a semester of tutoring, I was hired to lead the Peer-Led Tutoring Lessons (PLTL) sessions for my Physics 2 professor.

It was my first experience lecturing — standing in front of a classroom and teaching for an hour. I made lesson plans, practiced every morning, and felt the nerves and the imposter syndrome like a true leader.

## Process

I found that, like the lessons from the programming experience I was beginning to gain, and the lessons learned from tutoring, there were two common lessons:
1. there is nothing new under the sun 
2. learning the illusion of “genius” being perspective, liberates.

I taught students how I personally learned:

1. **Discover the wording of the question you are actually trying to ask.**
2. **Understand the perspective of the original innovator who asked it.**
3. **See the beauty.**

Physics2PLTL was a site dedicated to that process. On it I built a logic tree that took students from the homework I assigned to the answers, using the same resources I had used to get there.

Take flux, torque, current, gravitational attraction. As a list of equations, they are overwhelming. But once you learn that each of those calculations came out of a *context* — and that out from perspective from the science that came before it — a student can sit inside the point of view of Newton or Ampère and see the real beauty of science, and of the equation distilled from it.

## Result

All of my students passed Physics 2.

The site kept drawing traffic for years afterward, and I received a couple of emails from strangers thanking me for the resources.

Around 2019 I stopped keeping up with the Squarespace account, and the site was seemingly purged. Rebuilding that logic tree here is on my list.
