## Background and inspiration

During my Physics 2 honors class, I took it upon myself to complete a dream my brother and I had talked about: *let's build a fusion reactor.*

At Miami Dade College, honors courses are evaluated with a capstone project — one that demonstrates mastery of the course material and challenges students to step up to the plate on their own interests in it. This was the excuse I needed.

Fusion was one of the bundle of "human" problems I took an interest in early in my educational journey. As I wrote in the background to [IoT TFNA](/science/iot-two-factor-neurometric-authentication/), it was also an apparent prerequisite for the dream of building a mecha. Building one that simply *worked*, as exposure to the disciplines involved, was more than enough for a capstone.

## Process

I took my inspiration from the design published in *Make:* magazine, Vol. 36 — producing the CAD files, milling the materials, and building out the circuitry as required.

A Farnsworth fusor is an inertial electrostatic confinement device: a grid held at high negative voltage inside a vacuum chamber accelerates deuterium ions inward until some fraction of them collide hard enough to fuse. 

> A fusor operates at voltages and produces radiation that make it genuinely hazardous. Mine was built as coursework under supervision, and this post is a retrospective rather than a build guide.
